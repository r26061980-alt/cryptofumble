CryptoFumble

About

What is this thing?

CryptoFumble answers one question: what would your money be worth today if you'd bought, sold, or slowly stacked a coin at some point in the past? Pick a coin, an amount, and a date — it does the math against real historical prices. Sometimes the answer is thrilling. Usually it's a little painful. That's the point.

It's for fun and reflection, not investment advice. It shows a hypothetical outcome based on historical prices — never a prediction, never a recommendation. Crypto is volatile and you can lose money. Always do your own research before investing anything.

Track your real portfolio, not just hypotheticals

The calculator is for "what if" scenarios. "My Portfolio" is for real ones: log your actual purchases — coin, date, quantity, amount spent — and it tracks your real profit or loss against live prices, coin by coin and in total. Average price is worked out automatically from what you spent and how much you got, so it can't quietly drift out of sync.

A Telegram bot that keeps tabs on it for you

Connect your portfolio to @CryptoFumble_bot — one button on the site, one code sent to the bot, no separate account — and it sends a weekly digest straight to Telegram: how your portfolio moved, without you having to open the site to check. The bot can also run a calculation right inside the chat with /calc <coin> <amount> <year-month>, e.g. /calc btc 100 2020-01.

Who built it

CryptoFumble is an independent, one-person side project — not a company, not a fund, not a trading desk. It started from wanting to know exactly how much money past self left on the table, and turned into a small tool other people seem to enjoy torturing themselves with too.

Where the numbers come from

Live and historical prices come from CoinGecko, through a caching proxy so the site isn't hammering their API directly. If that's briefly unreachable, the calculator falls back to an approximate historical dataset for a handful of major coins — always clearly labeled "OFFLINE DEMO DATA" right on the result, so you know exactly what you're looking at. Numbers are rounded and illustrative; they won't match a trading terminal to the cent.

How it makes money

The calculator is free and will stay that way — no subscription, no paywalled export, no cap on how many times you can use it. If you liked it (or it was painful to look at your numbers) — there's an option right under the result to support the project with a direct crypto donation (BTC, ETH, or USDT), straight to the author's wallet, no middleman, no invoice, no fee taken off the top. It's always optional and never required to use the calculator. On top of that, some links to exchanges (like the "Start on an exchange" button after a result) are affiliate links — CryptoFumble may earn a small commission if you sign up through them, at no extra cost to you. That's disclosed right next to the link, not hidden in fine print.
